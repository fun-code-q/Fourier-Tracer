How it works
  1. Text. Glyphs come straight from the font as quadratic and cubic Bézier curves (opentype.js), flattened to sub-unit accuracy. No pixels are involved.
  2. Images. Pixels are grouped into main colours with k‑means++ in CIELAB. Marching squares follows each colour's edges, with points placed where a softened mask crosses 50% for sub-pixel accuracy.
  3. One path. All outlines are joined into one closed path with pen-up moves between them, in reading order for text and nearest-next for images. One Fourier series draws everything, so the main circle never moves.
  4. FFT. The path is resampled to 2ⁿ evenly spaced points, up to 262,144, and transformed with a radix-2 FFT in O(M log M). Each coefficient cₖ is a circle of radius |cₖ| turning k times per lap.
  5. Quality. Draft, HD and Ultra HD set the largest allowed gap as a share of the drawing height. A binary search over the circles, largest first, finds the fewest that meet it; each test is one inverse FFT.
  6. Guarantee. The gap can never exceed the sum of the radii left out. Lanczos σ-factors, σₖ = sinc(k/(m+1)), optionally damp Gibbs overshoot at corners.
  7. Output. Curves render as smooth quadratic splines at any zoom. SVG export simplifies with Ramer–Douglas–Peucker, then fits Catmull–Rom cubic Béziers.

Methods: Cooley–Tukey FFT (1965); elliptic Fourier descriptors (Kuhl & Giardina, 1982); Lanczos σ-factors (Applied Analysis, 1956); k-means++ (Arthur & Vassilvitskii, 2007) in CIELAB (CIE 1976); marching squares (Lorensen & Cline, 1987); Ramer–Douglas–Peucker (1972–73); Catmull–Rom splines (1974).
